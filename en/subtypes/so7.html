The shared core of Type 7

The following explanation is shared by all three instinctual subtypes of Type 7, drawn from our theory guide. The energy and function mappings are this site’s interpretive model.

7

Type 7: consumption that will not stop

  • Head · displacement (head → gut)
  • Passion: gluttony
  • ≈ Se (primary) / Ne

Type 7 belongs to the head center but avoids dwelling on serious questions, where deep reflection may encounter emptiness and pain. Energy sinks toward the gut, becoming a pursuit of pleasure, novelty and practical benefits. Gluttony names this endless consumption of opportunities, stimulation, plans and experiences, one after another, to avoid stopping.

Seven's perceiving function becomes clearer at subtype level. Self-preservation and social Sevens are grounded in reality: the former excels at benefits and enjoyment; the latter enjoys practical service and participation. Both strongly resemble extraverted sensation (Se). One-to-one Seven dreams of romance and unknown adventures, resembling extraverted intuition (Ne). With the same Ne, however, Eight breaks orders while Seven wanders and delights itself.

Seven is often mistakenly seen as naive or unintelligent. It can have exceptional improvisational ability and survival instinct while avoiding institutionally approved routes. Naruto Uzumaki in Naruto and Seong Gi-hun in Squid Game illustrate intelligence outside that framework. Here Seven resembles Five: both refuse to let external authority define their intelligence.

Sketch

Social Seven least resembles Seven. Others rush to enjoy; this subtype rushes to give. Warm, optimistic and idealistic, they postpone personal needs for a group or better world. Gluttony is hard to recognize at first.

Keyword: Sacrifice

Ichazo used sacrifice. Naranjo explained that what is sacrificed is personal greed, sending desires away for an ideal. Enjoying or benefiting personally causes guilt, while serving others creates a feeling of purity. His playful image was someone aware of an inner greedy pig and determined to become an unmoved pig.

Passion × instinct

Sacrifice has another side: So7 needs it witnessed and hopes to be called good. Gluttony shifts from pleasure to affection, praise and a pure self-image. Naranjo cited Saint Francis, warning that excessive selflessness can become overexertion and some good acts watch the audience from the corner of the eye. This model sees Seven's energy sinking toward the gut and social instinct conflicting with personal will, producing the countertype. Se remains prominent, finding pleasure in practical service and participation rather than direct enjoyment.

Everyday patterns

  • Is cheerful and volunteers for tasks others avoid.
  • Holds almost purist ideals about food, consumption, ecology or charity.
  • Struggles to state personal wants, considering them selfish.
  • Gains goodwill through helping and cares about being seen as good.
  • Has many plans and ideals but avoids conflict and negative feeling.

Commonly confused types

Helping resembles Two, which focuses on particular people and being needed; So7 focuses on ideals and a good-person image with a lighter atmosphere. Purity and world improvement resemble One, but One's background is anger and criticism while So7's is optimism and avoidance.

Blind spots and growth

The blind spot is denying desire and self-interest and using the collective good to avoid pain. Growth requires honesty: acknowledge wanting and dissatisfaction, distinguish heartfelt goodness from applause-seeking, and accept pain and conflict as reality. Doing less but more sincerely can make enthusiasm last.
